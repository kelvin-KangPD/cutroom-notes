The AI ad model is way too handsome: jumping in early, in the age of “AI? Fun is enough”

Also in: Bahasa Indonesia · हिन्दी · العربية · ไทย

We've reached the point where people hit 2.44 million hearts on an AI video, and my company got on that wave early and is making ads with AI models.

The ad model is way too handsome

Lately our team makes ads with AI models. But every time we make one and look at it, he's way too handsome.

A 5-second cut made with Higgsfield Motion Control (Kling 3.0). We put an AI-made character over footage of real, filmed movements

Handsome AIs like these are being mass-produced right now. It means we're not the only ones making them; everyone is. That's why speed matters.

The mood changed in one year

AI-generated content used to be a bad thing. People called it something churned out with one click, a devil taking our jobs. But after a year of that, the mood changed.

Past reaction Reaction now
Things made by AI Bad, churned out with one click Fun is enough
Cute AI videos Creepy Cute is enough
Funny AI videos Doesn't look real Funny is enough
Comments Arguing about whether it's AI or not Comments like that disappeared long ago

For a short while people rejected AI like a machine-smashing movement, and then they quickly moved over to accepting it.

The cat has five fingers and nobody says it's strange. People actually pressed 2.44 million hearts. It's now natural for a city hall to post an AI video on its official account. And this stuff keeps getting mass-produced.

Now is YouTube in 2015

I think back to the great YouTube boom of 2015 and 2016. The people who got established then gained 500,000 or a million subscribers just by standing out a little.

I think AI content is in exactly that spot now. People no longer reject something because it's AI. If it's fun, they watch. And all it costs to make is one click.

When Situation
A year ago AI was the devil. Make it and you get cursed at
Now Fun is enough. Not everyone has jumped in yet
Soon Everyone clicks. Hard to stand out much

In yesterday's post I said we have to claim our spot before content pours out without limit. Hearts pouring onto AI content means the starting gun for that race has already fired.

My company is already running

So my company didn't wait. While everyone was watching to see whether AI would be accepted, it went into AI ads first, and by making and fixing things quickly it is drawing good responses.

Polishing an ad made with an AI model in Premiere. MCP Bridge is connected at the top left, so the AI works Premiere directly

Trend What we do
AI models are being mass-produced We make ads with AI models too. We do shoot for real, and put an AI-generated character over that footage
People have accepted AI We don't hold back worrying about pushback; we watch the reaction now and publish right away
Trends change by the day When a new tool comes out, we switch over right away. We also connected Premiere so the AI can work it directly

You can tell how fast things change just from the tools we use. Last week we used Higgsfield's Edit Video, and this week we use Motion Control. There was originally a different menu in Motion Control's spot (I think it was Zenjutsu), and that changed again.

Edit Video last week, Motion Control this week

Trends change day by day. New tools keep coming out, and video quality keeps getting better.

To pull all this off, your hands have to be fast. Our team is working at that speed right now. And the company chose to get on board with the change first instead of blocking it.

Conclusion

The time of rejection was shorter than I expected. While people were watching to see whether AI would be accepted, it had already been accepted.

Anyone can click. So the difference comes not from what you make but from who does it first. We are standing on the side that goes first.
